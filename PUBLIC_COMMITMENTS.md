# CharityGraph public commitments

**Status:** Canonical public commitments

**Version:** 2.0-draft

**Date:** 24 August 2026

CharityGraph is the one-stop shop for structured, governed Australian charity data.

We commit to:

1. **Broad usefulness.** We will pursue national reach and progressively useful coverage rather than limit publication to a small set with forensic-level enrichment.
2. **Transparent origins.** Published data will identify or resolve to its source, scope, time, production method and release.
3. **Clear judgment labels.** We will distinguish authority-reported facts, first-party claims, independent reports, direct observations, calculations, taxonomy mappings and model-assessed judgments.
4. **Evidence-grounded model use.** LLMs will be used for the semantic judgment they are designed to perform. Their prompts, model and policy versions, inputs, structured outputs, costs and lineage will be governed and retained appropriately.
5. **Visible uncertainty without reflexive abstention.** We will disclose uncertainty and alternatives while still making useful low-risk classifications when evidence supports them.
6. **Coverage disclosure.** We will describe what sources and claim families were attempted, processed, reviewed and published. Missing data will not be represented as a negative fact.
7. **Neutral editorial policy.** We will not sell favourable rankings, treat commercial performance claims as equivalent to regulator-filed facts, or publish universal charity-worth scores.
8. **Contestability.** People will be able to challenge records, provide evidence and track review. Review may amend, qualify, withdraw or uphold a record.
9. **Visible correction.** Published releases remain immutable. Corrections will carry lineage and appear in later releases and release notes.
10. **Open curation.** We will use community scrutiny to improve data and methods while protecting the dataset from voting, brigading, conflicts of interest and direct ungoverned mutation.
11. **Right-tail fairness.** We will not infer poor quality, inactivity or illegitimacy from a charity’s small size, local focus, volunteer-led structure or limited digital footprint.
12. **Taxonomy transparency.** We will document external schemes considered and whether they were adopted, profiled, incorporated, crosswalked, adapted, deferred, rejected or superseded.
13. **Privacy and safety.** We will minimise personal information and protect vulnerable people, sensitive relationships, precise protected locations and restricted source material.
14. **Indigenous data governance.** Where applicable, we will respect community authority, cultural safety, benefit, access control and appropriate attribution.
15. **Stable open releases.** Public releases will have clear identity, schemas, licences, attribution, checksums and catalogue metadata.
16. **Simple public access.** Ordinary users will receive understandable organisation, program, service and evidence views even where the underlying model is sophisticated.
17. **Open analytical methods.** We will publish governed, reusable Playbooks so people can apply CharityGraph Data with the general-purpose AI system they already use.
18. **Versioned and corrigible methods.** Official Playbooks will be identifiable, versioned, attributable and open to evidence-based correction; Community Playbooks will remain distinct from official methods.
19. **Provider-neutral analysis.** Playbooks will not require ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or another named AI provider, and we will keep the Playbook method, a user's invocation and external-model output distinct.
20. **Private parameters and bounded outputs.** Where practical, user-supplied strategic parameters can remain in the user's invocation. Downstream analysis is not canonical CharityGraph knowledge merely because it used an official Playbook.

These commitments do not promise complete or real-time knowledge about every charity, a donation or volunteer marketplace, personal advice, universal rankings, hosted AI inference, or unrestricted publication of every acquired source document.

### Semantic transparency clarification

Explainability means reconstructible production provenance: governed source inputs, evidence bundles, task/prompt/policy identity, provider/model metadata, structured outputs, validation and release lineage. It does not promise internal chain-of-thought or lexical-causation explanations, and CharityGraph will not request or publish provider chain-of-thought.
