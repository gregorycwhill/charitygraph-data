# CharityGraph principles

**Status:** Canonical product principles

**Version:** 2.1-draft

**Date:** 28 August 2026

## 1. Be useful at sector scale

CharityGraph prefers broad, useful and governed coverage over forensic treatment of a tiny subset. Reach, claim-family coverage, accuracy, provenance and cost are balanced explicitly through cohort and claim-risk policies.

## 2. Preserve a provenance floor; tier forensic depth

Every published assertion retains source, scope, method, time, task/policy and release lineage. Expensive source breadth, evidence spans, corroboration, repeated model work and human review are allocated according to consequence and ambiguity.

## 3. Use models to exercise judgment

Deterministic code handles stable syntax and exact rules. Models handle bounded semantic interpretation. Humans handle high-consequence ambiguity and governance.

Do not teach Python to approximate open-ended language understanding through accumulating exceptions. Do not make a capable model abstain merely because a reasonable person might choose an adjacent low-risk classification.

The Semantic Heuristic Gate is ex-ante: before implementation, ask **Does this diff teach Python English?** Any deterministic interpretation of unrestricted language requires a specific approved exception recorded in SEMANTIC_HEURISTIC_APPROVALS.md. Without that approval, change the evidence, prompt, task schema, model, routing, benchmark or governance instead of adding phrase rules.

## 4. Preserve evidence before synthesis

Source-native evidence, extraction, mapping, adjudication and narrative are different layers. A synthesis is not evidence for itself.

## 5. Treat knowledge as assertions, not a mutable truth row

CharityGraph stores provenance-bearing observations and assertions, then records governed decisions and current views. Earlier records, conflicts and corrections remain reconstructible.

## 6. Put facts at the scope supported by evidence

Program, service, campaign, site and partner facts do not automatically become organisation-wide facts. Legal purpose does not prove current activity. Relationships do not propagate ethos, mandate, conduct, registration, outcomes or capacity.

## 7. Separate source facts, normalisation and judgment

Retain what a source actually reported. Record CharityGraph mappings, calculations and model assessments separately. A mapped concept does not imply that the source used that taxonomy.

## 8. Authority is claim-specific

A source can be authoritative for one claim and weak for another. Regulator facts, audited statements, first-party positions, independent evaluations and direct observations occupy different legitimate roles.

## 9. Be decisive without pretending infallibility

Ordinary ambiguity should yield a best-supported judgment, confidence, alternatives and evidence. `Unknown` is for genuinely insufficient evidence or failed processing—not for routine semantic caution.

## 10. Make absence explicit

Distinguish asserted none, observed absent, not found, source silent, unavailable, not acquired, not processed, failed, not reviewed, not applicable, withheld, stale and unknown.

Missing data has no negative meaning.

## 11. Use multiple taxonomies for distinct semantic jobs

Legal purpose, cause, population, operational activity, participation, fundraising, finance, geography, outcome and SDG alignment are different facets. No universal taxonomy or label union replaces them.

## 12. Publish taxonomy and source decisions

Researchers and sector insiders deserve to know what existing schemes were considered, incorporated, adapted, crosswalked, deferred or rejected and why.

## 13. Avoid universal scores

Do not publish one charity-quality, trust, ethos, impact, efficiency, reputation, controversy or truth score. Publish structured evidence and transparent derived measures where comparison is legitimate.

## 14. Protect right-tail organisations

Small, local, volunteer-led, seasonal, culturally specific and lightly documented charities must not be penalised for limited digital footprint, sparse reporting, no Wikipedia article or informal operating models.

## 15. Make complexity serve simplicity

The governed model may be sophisticated for developers, agents and LLMs. Ordinary users receive clear projections and progressive disclosure. Do not simplify the public experience by corrupting internal semantics.

## 16. Treat publication as a governed projection

The public product is not an archive dump, runtime database or model cache. Knowledge acceptance and publication eligibility are separate decisions.

## 17. Keep releases immutable and corrections visible

Never rewrite a published release. Correct through new records, directed lineage, release notes and later releases. Emergency protected-data withdrawal may restrict access without erasing safe catalogue history.

## 18. Design for contestability

Every material public assertion should be challengeable. Review may change, qualify, withdraw or uphold it. Community correction is a success pathway, not an embarrassment to suppress.

## 19. Evidence, not voting, governs community contributions

Open curation does not mean direct public mutation or majority rule. Contributors supply evidence and criticism; CharityGraph applies transparent claim-family standards and manages conflicts of interest.

## 20. Minimise personal and sensitive data

Public Data is aggregate-first for donors, beneficiaries, participants, volunteers and frontline workers. Protect precise sensitive locations, informal relationships, complainants, survivors and small-community identities.

## 21. Respect rights and Indigenous governance

Public accessibility is not reuse permission. Copyright, database rights, privacy, ethics, cultural authority, attribution and Indigenous data sovereignty constrain acquisition and publication.

## 22. Measure economics and failure honestly

Track reach, coverage, false promotion, abstention, review load, corrections, model cost and subgroup performance. Do not optimise a metric by processing only easy, prominent or well-documented subjects.

## 23. Fix error classes, not individual examples

Classify failures before changing code. Add a rule only for a recurring defensible class or a deliberately supported high-value source. Preserve singleton ambiguity as evidence, review or an explicit limitation.

## 24. Reality-test before freezing public contracts

Use representative charities and recovered evidence to test scope, model economics, right-tail bias and review burden before committing to a public vNext schema.

## 25. Keep the project open and independently reusable

Public code, data, schemas, methods and release identity permit inspection, reuse and challenge. Open licensing does not grant others the right to imply endorsement or misuse CharityGraph branding.

## 26. Measure validity and reliability separately

CharityGraph measures whether semantic judgments are supported by evidence and whether materially identical semantic measurements reproduce similar graph structure. Repeatability is evidence about reliability, not truth; a consistently reproduced error remains an error.

Valid persisted semantic results are durable inputs to reproducible releases. Unchanged model tasks are not routinely regenerated. A different stochastic output does not mechanically establish real-world change, and publisher/source role/authority remains part of interpreting evidence. A source interface or metadata artefact is not automatically an activity of the subject being described.

## 27. Extend the commons from data to analytical capability

CharityGraph may publish open, governed Playbooks so people can apply reusable methods to governed Data with the general-purpose AI system they already use. This extends access to analytical capability without turning CharityGraph into a hosted inference platform.

## 28. Keep methods, invocations and outputs separate

A versioned Playbook definition is governed CharityGraph content. A parameterised invocation and the output produced in an external AI environment are separate artefacts and do not become canonical knowledge merely through use of an official Playbook.

## 29. Keep Playbooks model- and provider-neutral

Official methods SHALL remain portable across general-purpose AI providers. They should state evidence, scope, uncertainty, limitations and retrieval expectations without depending on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or another named provider.

## Semantic extraction and mapping guardrails

- Capture supported governed knowledge before downstream compression or synthesis.
- Source provenance does not imply proposition ownership; attach facts at the
  lowest evidence-supported subject or scope.
- Funding is not delivery, and operating, sponsoring, partnering, auspicing and
  network/context roles remain distinct.
- Inferred semantic lenses use taxonomy-blind governed knowledge views and are
  independent of source-reported taxonomy contamination.
- Representation failure, truncation or a placeholder is not evidence absence;
  representation gaps remain explicit.

## 30. Make method contribution open, evidence-governed and privacy-aware

People may suggest, contribute, review and correct Playbooks through a low-friction path that does not require GitHub, YAML, JSON or programming. Governance, evidence and contribution lineage—not popularity or voting—determine official status. User-supplied strategic parameters should remain private or minimised where practical.
